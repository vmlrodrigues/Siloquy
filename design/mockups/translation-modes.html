Translation across dictation languages

Today a translation tile has a fixed destination, so “To Portuguese” is still sitting there — greyed out and useless — while you are speaking Portuguese. In every option below that tile is gone rather than dimmed, and translation means out of the language you are speaking. They differ in where the destination comes from and how many ⌘ slots it costs.

A · MirrorNo configuration

The languages you dictate in are the languages you translate between. One tile, and its destination flips to whichever other language you have. Nothing to set up and nothing to keep in sync — adding Português as a dictation language gives you translation both ways for free.

ForZero setup. ⌘3 always means “translate”, in both directions. One list to manage instead of two.
AgainstYou can only translate into a language you also dictate in — no “to Mandarin” unless you add Mandarin as a dictation language. With three languages it needs two tiles, not one.
B · Destination listClosest to today

Keep an explicit list of destinations, as now, but include your dictation languages in it and hide whichever one you are currently speaking. The greyed tile simply disappears and the others close the gap.

ForTranslate into anything, including languages you never speak. Smallest change from what ships today.

C · One Translate keyTwo steps for a second destination

A single Translate tile that keeps one ⌘ slot no matter how many destinations you have. It remembers the last destination you used from the language you are speaking, so the common case is still one keystroke; pressing and holding opens the list.

What A looks like with four dictation languages

English, Português, Español, Français. Every mode needs a tile for the three languages you are not speaking, so translation grows to three tiles and the ordering question stops being academic.
